# Language Polishing & Logic Optimization

## 1. Eliminating "Chinglish" & Wordiness

### Directness
- **Avoid**: "It is widely known that..." (Too wordy)
- **Use**: "Research shows that..." or just cite the source directly.
- **Avoid**: "Make an improvement to..."
- **Use**: "Improve."

### Precision
- **Instead of "big"**: Use "substantial," "considerable," or "extensive."
- **Instead of "good"**: Use "effective," "efficacious," or "superior."
- **Instead of "bad"**: Use "adverse," "deleterious," or "detrimental."

## 2. Logical Flow & Transitions

### The "Old-to-New" Information Flow
- Start sentences with information that is already known to the reader (old) and end with new information.
- **Example**: "The drug inhibits enzyme X. This inhibition leads to..."

### Signposting
- Use transition words to guide the reader:
    - **Sequence**: "First," "Next," "Finally."
    - **Concession**: "Admittedly," "While it is true that..."
    - **Emphasis**: "Notably," "Importantly," "Crucially."

## 3. Vocabulary Diversity

Avoid repeating the same verb or noun in every sentence.

| Concept | Synonyms |
|---------|----------|
| Suggest | Propose, imply, indicate, postulate |
| Result | Outcome, finding, consequence, effect |
| Method | Approach, procedure, technique, strategy |
| Increase | Augment, escalate, amplify, expand |

## 4. Grammar & Style Check

- **Subject-Verb Agreement**: Watch out for collective nouns (e.g., "The data *are*" vs "The data *is*"). In science, "data" is usually plural.
- **Dangling Modifiers**: Ensure the subject of the modifier matches the subject of the main clause.
    - **Incorrect**: "Using this method, the results were obtained." (The results didn't use the method).
    - **Correct**: "Using this method, we obtained the results."
- **Serial Comma (Oxford Comma)**: Use it for clarity in lists (e.g., "A, B, and C").
