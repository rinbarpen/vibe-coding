# English Academic Writing Guide (Writer Mode)

## 1. IMRaD Structure Standards

### Introduction: The "Why"
- **Move 1: Establishing a territory**: Cite recent literature to show the importance of the research area.
- **Move 2: Establishing a niche**: Identify a gap, problem, or unanswered question ("However, it remains unclear whether...").
- **Move 3: Occupying the niche**: State the purpose of your study ("In this study, we aimed to...").

### Methods: The "How"
- **Tense**: Use **Past Tense** for actions completed ("Patients were randomized...").
- **Voice**: Use **Passive Voice** to emphasize the action, but **Active Voice** is increasingly accepted for clarity ("We measured..." vs "Measurements were taken").
- **Detail**: Must be sufficient for replication. Include ethical approval and statistical software versions.

### Results: The "What"
- **Past Tense**: Report findings ("The mean age was...").
- **Objectivity**: Do not interpret results here. Use tables/figures to support text.
- **Precision**: Report exact P-values (e.g., P = .023) rather than just P < .05.

### Discussion: The "So What"
- **Comparison**: Compare your results with previous studies ("Our findings are consistent with...").
- **Mechanism**: Propose explanations for your findings.
- **Limitations**: Acknowledge weaknesses (sample size, follow-up duration).
- **Conclusion**: Summarize clinical implications.

## 2. Tense and Voice Conventions

| Section | Common Tense | Purpose |
|---------|--------------|---------|
| Introduction | Present / Present Perfect | General truths / Recent research |
| Methods | Past | Completed actions |
| Results | Past | Specific findings |
| Discussion | Present | Interpreting results / General conclusions |

## 3. Academic Vocabulary & Phrases

- **Instead of "get"**: Use "obtain," "acquire," or "elicit."
- **Instead of "show"**: Use "demonstrate," "illustrate," or "reveal."
- **Transitions**:
    - Addition: "Furthermore," "Moreover," "In addition."
    - Contrast: "Conversely," "Nevertheless," "On the contrary."
    - Causality: "Consequently," "Therefore," "Hence."

## 4. Avoiding Common Pitfalls
- **No Contractions**: Use "do not" instead of "don't."
- **Avoid "Very"**: Use more precise adjectives (e.g., "substantial," "significant").
- **Parallelism**: Ensure lists follow the same grammatical structure.
