# English Peer Review Standards (Reviewer Mode)

## 1. Reviewer Report Structure

A professional peer review report should follow this structure:

### Summary of the Research
- Briefly state what the paper is about and its main contribution.
- "This study investigates the efficacy of [X] in [Y] patients. The authors found that..."

### Major Comments (Critical Issues)
- **Scientific Validity**: "The sample size is too small to support the conclusion that..."
- **Methodological Flaws**: "The randomization process is not clearly described, which raises concerns about potential bias."
- **Statistical Errors**: "The use of a t-test is inappropriate for this non-normally distributed data."

### Minor Comments (Technical/Formatting Issues)
- **Clarity**: "The sentence on line 45 is ambiguous and should be rephrased."
- **Typos/Grammar**: "There are several grammatical errors throughout the manuscript (e.g., lines 12, 88)."
- **References**: "Citation [15] is outdated; consider citing the more recent study by Smith et al. (2024)."

## 2. Critical Appraisal Checklist

- **Originality**: Does this study provide new insights?
- **Abstract**: Does the abstract accurately reflect the content of the paper?
- **Figures/Tables**: Are they self-explanatory? Do they match the text?
- **Ethics**: Is there a clear statement on informed consent and IRB approval?
- **Conclusion**: Is it over-reaching? Does it stay within the bounds of the data?

## 3. Recommendation Categories

- **Accept**: No changes needed (rare).
- **Minor Revision**: Small changes, mostly related to clarity or formatting.
- **Major Revision**: Significant issues with data analysis, interpretation, or study design.
- **Reject**: Fatal flaws in methodology or lack of novelty.

## 4. Professional Tone in Reviews
- Be constructive, not insulting.
- Use "The authors should consider..." instead of "The authors failed to..."
- Use "It would be helpful to see..." instead of "I want to see..."
